01 / THE CENTRAL IDEA

One view gives a line.
A new view gives a point.

A single configuration cannot distinguish contact points along the force line of action. A narrow particle cloud can look certain while missing the true contact.

Keep the ambiguity that the measurement cannot remove.

APCL restores hypotheses along the unresolved direction. Reorienting the grasped object then changes the force direction in the object frame, making the contact point identifiable across views.

(p + αf) × f = p × f

The same force and moment can correspond to an entire line of contact points.

03 / THE METHOD

Measure what is observable.
Preserve what is not.

APCL combines residual validation, ambiguity-preserving contact inference, and active measurement. The object geometry and contact location are unknown.

A

Validate the residual

Estimate the payload contribution, then check the compensated momentum-observer residual on separate challenge poses.

Calibration + trust gate
B

Preserve hypotheses

The core recovery rule, D3, re-supplies hypotheses along the unresolved force line. Metropolis–Hastings moves and auxiliary recovery rules support the particle estimate.

Line-of-action recovery
C

Choose the next view

Score feasible orientations with profile Fisher information. Change orientation while regulating the tool position, then update the estimate.

Active reorientation

Preserve the alternatives.
Then separate them.

The comparison isolates what recovery adds to a CPF that already uses Metropolis–Hastings (MH) moves. Recovery reduces confident but wrong estimates; active view selection resolves ambiguity with fewer measurements.

Confident but wrong (CBW): reported 95% particle radius < 10 mm while true position error > 20 mm. All methods select views actively. The regularized PF uses kernel roughening; the other variants use MH moves and differ in recovery. Its archived baseline uses the same seeds and calibration states. Ellipsoid coverage uses the covariance-based region; particle-ball coverage checks whether the true error is within the reported radius.

Errors above 20 mm: 8.0% without recovery and 5.7% with APCL.

APCL: 0 CBW events in 1,200 trials. The exact 95% interval is 0.00–0.31%; zero observed events do not establish zero risk.

What the additional studies test.

Across all 1,200 trials, line-of-action recovery alone (D3) reached a p95 error of 20.7 mm with 0 CBW events, similar to the complete method. D1 and D2 are auxiliary rules; these results do not establish an additional accuracy gain from combining all three.

A known load as a reference.

A second completed study attaches a steel disc after payload calibration: eight mounting positions, three masses (0.5, 1.0, and 1.5 kg), four repetitions, and three simulated robot instances — 288 trials.

The target is the disc’s center of mass, where its weight acts as an equivalent point force. This tests the measurement chain against a known reference; it does not model a surface-contact experiment.
